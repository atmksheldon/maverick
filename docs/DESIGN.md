## Problem Statement

There is no global index of internet-connected robots. Shodan and Censys cover internet-exposed devices in general, but their robot coverage is incidental — search for "Roomba" on Shodan and you get inconsistent, undercount results. Robot OEMs publish unit counts in marketing material but no neutral observatory exists. Security researchers writing about robot vulnerabilities (Roomba camera CVEs, Eufy MQTT leaks, Unitree exposed SDK ports) currently roll their own scanners every paper.

MAVERICK is that neutral observatory: a Shodan/Censys-style discovery service scoped specifically to robots — vacuums, humanoids, lawn bots, telepresence, delivery bots, industrial arms. Open-source, research-flavored, with a live world map, Shodan-style query DSL, and (eventually) a live telemetry stream.

The name is the metaphor: every "maverick" is an unbranded calf strayed from the herd — every device the system finds is a robot loose on the internet nobody has claimed or counted.

## What Makes This Cool

- **The launch screenshot.** A planet-scale map with hundreds of thousands of vacuum dots and a separate "Humanoid Atlas" pane cataloging every known Figure / 1X / Unitree / Agility / Tesla endpoint. One image tells the entire story: "you didn't know they were this many; here is each one."
- **First-mover on the category.** Robot-as-a-class internet observatory does not exist. Owning the category early means cited-by-default in every robot-security paper for the next decade.
- **Three audiences served from one dataset.** Security researchers (exposure stats), ROS / robotics researchers (fleet observability), robot OEMs (ground truth on their own deployed populations) — same data, three views.
- **Cowboy metaphor lands.** "Round up the mavericks" is the home page, "Brand" is the catalog action, "Strays" tags devices that wandered out of expected network segments, "Trailhead" is the dashboard landing. The brand voice is built in.

## Constraints

- **Solo build.** Other active projects (RanchGuard, WellStride, Zinkwell, multiple shadenode services). MAVERICK must be operable as a part-time research project, not a full-time job.
- **Budget: ~$60/mo all-in.** 3x Hetzner CAX11 ARM VPS for scanners (~$13/mo) + shadenode (already paid) + Mapbox (already keyed) + free passive feeds. No paid Shodan / Censys tiers.
- **Ethical posture is load-bearing.** This must not become a doxxing tool or an exploit catalog. Brand survives only if every artifact (paper, blog post, dashboard) reinforces the research framing.
- **Time-to-demo: 8 weeks.** The project compounds from a public demo, not from heads-down stealth.

## Premises (locked)

1. **Active scanning is permitted.** Masscan-based sweeps for known robot signatures. Slow rate (~1000 pps), not aggressive. Source IPs serve a research-charter page on `/about`.
2. **Existence + fingerprint, never contents.** Index that a device exists at approximate location with model + firmware. **Never** log floorplans, video, PII, credentials, or attempt exploitation. **Never** publish street-level coordinates — reverse-geocode to city level only.
3. **Two-tier data release.** Aggregate stats, fingerprint definitions, and the public index are open. Raw scan data and per-device records are restricted (researcher-only with vetting). Prevents the project from being a doxxing tool.
4. **Hosted on shadenode.** Collector + index + dashboard run on shadenode behind Cloudflare Tunnel. Scanners are external Hetzner Cloud VPS to keep the home IP off blocklists.
5. **120 days to public launch.** Vacuum + humanoid panes, both live, with research charter and launch post ready.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Active-first scanner (rejected)
ZMap/Masscan-heavy from day one across many ports. Highest data quality, four months heads-down before any demo. Rejected for solo-build abandonment risk.

### Approach B: Passive-only aggregation (rejected)
Pull only from Shodan / Censys / InternetDB. Ships in 8 weeks but permanently limited to other people's coverage; weak original-research claim. Rejected for thin map.

### Approach C: Cost-aware hybrid (selected)
- **Narrow active scanning from day one.** Masscan on 3x Hetzner CAX11 in different DCs (Falkenstein, Hillsboro, Helsinki) at ~1000 pps with research charter at `/about`. Target only the highest-signal ports: miIO UDP 54321, ROS XML-RPC 11311, Unitree 8080/8081, Tuya MQTT 1883/8883, Eufy MQTT.
- **Free-tier passive enrichment.** Censys academic tier (request access), Shodan's free InternetDB endpoint, GitHub code search for leaked configs, public MQTT broker lists.
- **Two demoable panes from day one.** Population view (vacuum density map) + Humanoid Atlas (curated card per humanoid endpoint).
- **Community fingerprints by month 4.** Open the device-signature definitions as a contributable repo. MAVERICK becomes the curator, not the sole researcher.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C, four-phase build:**

- **Weeks 1-2: Foundations.** Postgres + PostGIS on shadenode. Cloudflare Tunnel routed to `maverick.{domain}.tld` (TBD). Charter page deployed on each Hetzner scanner. Schema for `devices`, `fingerprints`, `observations`, `sources`.
- **Weeks 3-4: First scanners.** Masscan from one Hetzner CAX11 sweeping miIO UDP 54321 and Tuya MQTT. Postgres ingest pipeline. Internal-only dashboard at `/admin` showing first hits.
- **Weeks 5-6: Map + DSL.** Mapbox dashboard with vacuum density layer. Shodan-style query DSL parser (`vendor:roborock firmware:>4.0 country:de`). Internal-only humanoid catalog seeded from public sources.
- **Weeks 7-8: Soft beta + Humanoid Atlas.** Three scanners running. Free passive feeds wired in. Humanoid Atlas pane live. Public launch with research charter, blog post, and HN submission.
- **Weeks 9-16: Coverage + community.** Add Roborock cloud, Eufy MQTT, Unitree SDK fingerprints. Open the fingerprint repo for community PRs. Submit research note to academic venue (USENIX SOUPS, ACSAC, or arXiv).

## Open Questions

- **Domain choice.** `maverick.io` vs `maverick.dev` vs `findmaverick.com` vs `maverick-index.org`. Check availability and pick before week 1.
- **Research-affiliation cover.** Solo independent researcher is defensible but weaker than university affiliation. Worth pursuing an informal partnership with a CS security group (e.g. someone doing IoT research at a university you have contact with)?
- **Censys academic tier eligibility.** Confirm whether independent open-source researchers qualify, or if a university affiliation is required.
- **GDPR / privacy regulator exposure.** EU vacuums with location data may invoke GDPR even though we only store city-level. Need a legal review by month 3 — DPO question for EU-based researchers. Probably handled by city-level reverse-geocoding + clear opt-out, but document it.
- **Humanoid endpoints — is there a reachable surface in 2026?** Most humanoid fleets are on private/research networks. Need to validate at week 1 that there are >10 publicly reachable humanoid endpoints worldwide to make the Atlas pane meaningful at launch.
